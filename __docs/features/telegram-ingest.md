# Telegram import

The `Ingest` module reads the lost-and-found channels and groups our Telegram account follows, turns real notices into site notices and reposts them to our own channel. It also mirrors into the channel the notices created on the site and in the admin panel, and deletes a channel post once its notice is archived or deleted (see [Notices from the site](#notices-from-the-site)). Public Facebook groups are read too, through a logged-in account once an hour (see [Ingest](../architecture/modules/ingest.md#facebook)); from the phone step on, a Facebook post goes the same way. Notices created this way are ordinary notices: matching in `Worker` and claims work for them unchanged.

## The flow

One post at a time, from an in-memory queue (`IngestQueue` → `IngestPipeline`):

1. **Listen.** `TdlightTelegramSource` (TDLight, a userbot session) takes every new text or photo message of the chats in the Telegram folder `TELEGRAM_FOLDER`. Messages of one sender (the forward origin, else the sender) that come within `TELEGRAM_ALBUM_WAIT` of each other and carry at most one text are merged into one post (`PostBuffer`, `PostGroup`): an album, but also a text with its photos sent as a separate message or album, which is how channel admins forward a person's notice. Each message therefore waits `TELEGRAM_ALBUM_WAIT` before it goes on; the merged post is keyed by its first message. The catch-up groups the history the same way. After a start it also replays the last `TELEGRAM_CATCH_UP_MAX_AGE` of every chat; posts already in `ingest_post` are dropped. `FacebookPostSource` polls the groups of `FACEBOOK_GROUPS` every `FACEBOOK_POLL_EVERY` plus a random jitter and hands over what is new since the last poll.
2. **Phone.** `PhoneExtractor` (libphonenumber, the chat's region) finds numbers in the text. No number in the text → the post's source looks for the author's phone; for Telegram `AuthorPhoneResolver` tries the phone the author made public in their Telegram profile: the sender in a group, the forward origin of a channel post, then users mentioned in the text (`@username` or a link to the user), because channel posts often name the author only as `Контакт: @username`. Still nothing → the source looks for the author's profile (`AuthorProfile`): for Telegram `AuthorProfileResolver` takes the first active `@username` of the sender or the forward origin (not of users mentioned in the text, not of bots), linked as `https://t.me/<username>`; for Facebook the author's profile name and link. The notice then carries no phone but `nickName` (cut to 50 characters), `profileUrl` (only `https`, up to 255) and that one network in `socialMedias`, `derechi.ingest.phone.source` counts it as `profile`, and a claim on it tells the claimant to write to the author there (see [Claims](claims.md#an-author-with-only-a-nickname)). No profile either → the post is dropped without a trace (`outcome=no_phone`).
3. **Duplicate.** The same contact number (HMAC in `ingest_post.phone_hash`; for a profile the HMAC of `<NETWORK>:<nickname in lower case>`) on a notice within `INGEST_DUPLICATE_WINDOW` → skipped. This runs before the model, so a repost in five channels costs one model call.
4. **Mask.** `Masker` replaces numbers with `[PHONE_n]` and emails with `[EMAIL_n]`; the mapping stays in memory. No phone or email leaves the machine.
5. **Redact photos.** Up to `INGEST_MAX_PHOTOS` photos are downloaded and redacted by `DefaultImageRedactor` before anything else sees them: Tesseract (`ukr+eng`) reads the photo and, when the lines it found are short (median under 40 px: a card in a phone photo gives it ~110 dpi, `Estimating resolution as 113` in the log, and the name on the card is lost), reads it again enlarged so the median line is 40 px tall (at most 4×; a close-up with large print is not enlarged, since huge text reads worse), with the boxes mapped back to the photo's own coordinates; lines with phone numbers or emails are blurred; a photo OCR reads badly as it is (fewer than ~120 confident characters) is also read turned by 90° and 270°, the way a document lying sideways is photographed, and when a turn reads clearly better (1.5× the upright reading, at least 20 confident characters) the whole photo is turned upright before the faces are looked for and anything is blurred, so the face is found and kept and the published photo is upright too; upside-down documents are not tried; a bank card number is blurred up to its last 4 digits; a document (keywords in the post or on the photo, an MRZ line, or a portrait with printed text around it: the largest face, found by YuNet through ONNX Runtime, with at least 3 OCR lines when it sits near the left edge and 6 anywhere else) is blurred everywhere except its faces, so the owner or relatives can recognize the person, and entirely when it has no face. These are guesses made before the model is asked, so the model sees nothing private; after it answers, a photo blurred as a document in a notice whose category is not `DOCUMENTS` is redacted again from the original on the photo's own evidence only (keywords or an MRZ line read by OCR, not the post text and not a portrait), which keeps a dog's face or a backpack "of a soldier" sharp. Every photo is re-encoded, which drops EXIF and GPS data. The redactor also keeps the lines OCR read before the blur (`RedactedImage.text`, filtered by `PhotoText`: a line with any digit, an `@`, a link or fewer than two letters is dropped, so document numbers, dates, addresses, card and tax numbers, emails and handles stay on the machine) for step 6a. At `DEBUG` the redactor logs the lines that may go to the model as they are and the dropped ones masked (digits as `#`, handles and links as `[contact]`), and the pipeline logs the exact list it sends, so a log shows what left the machine.
6. **Extract.** `OpenRouterExtractor` sends the masked text and the redacted photos to OpenRouter with a strict JSON schema (`prompts/extraction-schema.json`, the categories of Client-API patched in). `ExtractionPolicy` decides whether the answer is a usable notice and picks the contact number by the placeholder the model returned. For an animal the model puts its breed into the title ("Загублено собаку породи хаскі") when the post gives it or a photo clearly shows one breed; a mix or an unsure guess gets no breed. A found notice without a photo is skipped as `no_photo`, unless its category has a stand-in photo in `INGEST_PLACEHOLDER_DOCUMENTS` / `INGEST_PLACEHOLDER_WALLET`: then the notice is created with that storage key (`Notice.placeholderPhoto`, nothing is uploaded) and the channel post carries the picture from the bucket.
   - **6a. The owner's name.** The model answers `ownerName` when the post or a photo names the owner of a document, and puts it into the title. A document photo is blurred before the model sees it, so for a `DOCUMENTS` notice without `ownerName` the pipeline makes a second, text-only call (`OwnerNameFinder`, `prompts/owner-name.md`, `prompts/owner-name-schema.json`): the title and the numbered OCR lines of the photos (words only, see step 5), and the model says whether one of them is a person's name (also from an MRZ line) and returns the title with the name added in the title's language. A failed or unavailable call keeps the title and logs a `WARN`; the post is not retried for it. The call counts toward `OPENROUTER_DAILY_LIMIT` and the `derechi.ingest.llm.*` metrics.
7. **Place.** Google Places Text Search for the place text with the city or the chat's default city, then for the city alone. `GOOGLE_PLACES_API` picks the API: `LEGACY` (default, `GoogleLegacyPlaceResolver`, the country comes from the end of `formatted_address`, else the chat's region) or `NEW` (`GooglePlaceResolver`, Places API (New) with a field mask). Google's name of a place is a venue or a street ("MAGNUS", "проспект Свободи, 92"), so the stored name is Google's address, with the venue name in front unless the address already starts with it ("Магнус, вулиця Зелена, 100, Львів"): the formatted address (`formatted_address` with `LEGACY`, `formattedAddress` in the field mask with `NEW`). The country and everything after it is cut off, as the country is stored in its own field, and so are a region ("… область", "województwo …", "Автономна Республіка Крим") and a postal code (`76000`, `31-042`) left at the end. The whole name is cut to 100 characters. The model writes `placeText` as a map search query (official names instead of local slang, using the chat's city as `chatCity`). A failed search (an HTTP error or a non-`OK` status) skips the post as `no_place` with a `WARN`, it does not fail it.
8. **Publish.** The pipeline hands the ready notice to `Publishers`, which runs every publisher named in `INGEST_PUBLISHERS`:
   - `site` (`SitePublisher`) goes first: it puts the photo into MinIO (`S3ImageUploader`) and creates the notice through the public API entry, Caddy for now (`GraphQlDeRechiApi`, `createLostItem` / `createFoundItem`). Without `site` the post is recorded as previewed and nothing reaches the site.
   - `channel` (`ChannelPoster`, Bot API) posts to our channel. Its caption never carries contacts; it links to the notice page, where a reader leaves their contacts through a claim, so `channel` needs `site` in the list. The caption ends with hashtags, so a reader taps `#Львів` and sees only that city in the channel: the town (the model's `city`, a nominative name, else the chat's city; `Івано-Франківськ` becomes `#Івано_Франківськ`, as a hyphen ends a hashtag), the kind (`#Загублено` / `#Знайдено`) and the category (`#Ключі`, none for `OTHER`), all from `channel.hashtag.*` in the bundles. It keeps the id of its post in `ingest_post.message_id`, so the post can be deleted later.

   An empty `INGEST_PUBLISHERS` is a dry run, `site,channel` publishes. If `site` fails, nobody else is called; any other publisher failing only logs a `WARN`. A skipped post goes nowhere: its reason is the `Post … skipped: <outcome>` log line and the `outcome` tag of `derechi.ingest.posts`.

## Notices from the site

`Ingest` reads `ingest.queue` (`NoticeEventListener`, see [messaging](../architecture/messaging.md)): `notice.<kind>.created` from `Client-API` and `Admin-API`, `notice.<kind>.updated` from `Admin-API`, `notice.<kind>.archived` from `Worker` and `notice.<kind>.deleted` from `Admin-API`. `returned` is not bound: `Worker` archives a returned notice right away, and `archived` follows. `ChannelMirror` does the work only when `channel` is in `INGEST_PUBLISHERS` and the bot can post (`TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN`, `TELEGRAM_PUBLISH_CHAT`); otherwise the event is acknowledged and nothing happens.

- **Created.** Notices that `Ingest` itself published arrive here too, and `Client-API` sends the event before its GraphQL answer reaches `SitePublisher`, so the listener may get it before the item id is in `ingest_post`. While `ingest_post` holds a `PUBLISHING` row, the listener waits (checking every second, at most `INGEST_IN_FLIGHT_WAIT`, 90 s). Then a notice already in `ingest_post` is skipped (the pipeline posts its own, and a notice from the site posted before has its row); any other is posted from the event itself: title, description, place, date, reward (`0`, a reward by agreement, reads just "💰 Винагорода"), the kind and category hashtags (`categoryKey`) and the link to the notice (`DERECHI_SITE_URL` and `INGEST_SITE_LOCALE`). There is no city hashtag, since the API has no city. The photo is read from the bucket by its key (`StoredPhotos`), only `.jpg`, `.jpeg` and `.png`; any other format or a failed read posts the text alone.
- **Updated.** An edit in the admin panel edits the channel post in place, whichever side made it (the pipeline's own posts have their `message_id` too): `editMessageMedia` with the photo from the bucket and the new caption, `editMessageText` when the notice has no photo. Telegram refuses an edit that changes the kind of message (a photo added or removed) or touches a message it no longer lets the bot edit; then the old post is deleted and a new one made, and `message_id` is replaced. "Message is not modified" counts as done, so a redelivered event changes nothing. A notice without a channel message is ignored. The outcome is in `derechi.ingest.channel{action="edited"}`.
- **Archived or deleted.** The post is deleted with the Bot API's `deleteMessage` and `message_id` is cleared. Telegram promises deletion only for messages younger than 48 hours (in a channel where the bot may delete messages older ones usually go too): when it refuses, the post stays, `message_id` stays for cleaning up by hand, and the listener logs a `WARN` and counts `derechi.ingest.channel{action="deleted",outcome="failed"}`; the message is not retried.

The posts live in `ingest_post.message_id` (migration `014`). A notice from the pipeline keeps it on its own row. A notice from the site gets a row of its own once it is posted (`ChannelPoster.postFromSite`, status `PUBLISHED` with the message id): `source` is `site` or `admin` (the event's `origin`), `channel` is `internal` and `external_id` is the kind and the id (`lost/7`; a lost and a found notice may share an id). A failed post writes nothing, the event is retried and then dead-lettered into `ingest.dlq`; a crash between the post and the row posts the notice again when the event is redelivered. The admin panel links only `telegram` and `facebook` rows to their source, so `internal` rows show no link. Channel posts made before migration 014 have no `message_id` and are never deleted.

## `ingest_post`

One row per post that reached the model (migration `012`), keyed by source, channel and the post's id in it. No text, no phone number.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SKIPPED` | not published; the reason is the `outcome` tag of `derechi.ingest.posts` and the log line |
| `PREVIEWED` | `site` is not in `INGEST_PUBLISHERS` (a dry run): the notice went nowhere |
| `PUBLISHING` | committed right before the GraphQL call |
| `PUBLISHED` | the notice exists on the site; whether the channel post and the other publishers succeeded is in the log and `derechi.ingest.published` |
| `FAILED` | retries exhausted. Delete the row and the next start's catch-up takes the post again, if it is younger than the catch-up window |
| `NEEDS_CHECK` | found in `PUBLISHING` after a crash, or the GraphQL call ended unclearly: check on the site whether the notice exists. Never retried automatically |

A stage that is unavailable throws a `StageUnavailableException` and records nothing. The subclasses name the stage: `OpenRouterUnavailableException` (no key, or 429, 502 or 503, or a refused connection, after its own retries), `BudgetExceededException` (over `OPENROUTER_DAILY_LIMIT`), `ClientApiUnavailableException` (categories or countries unreachable), `PlacesUnavailableException` (no Google key); the Telegram client and Facebook throw the base class. `IngestQueue` catches the base class only: it puts the post aside (`derechi.ingest.postponed`) and queues it again at the exception's `retryAt()` (the next UTC midnight for the budget) or else 10 minutes later, at most 3 times, the budget included; then the post is dropped and only the next catch-up brings it back. A post that goes through starts its count anew, and the same post arriving meanwhile is not queued twice. The `outcome` tag comes from the exception: `budget` for the budget, `unavailable` for the rest. Other OpenRouter errors (500, a client error, an answer that is not valid twice) are `FAILED`. A restart forgets the postponed posts, but the catch-up brings them back, since the ledger has no row for them.

## Running it

- Telegram session, once per volume: stop the service (`docker compose ... stop ingest`: TDLib locks its database folder), run `docker compose -f docker-compose.yml -f docker-compose.services.yml run --rm -it ingest --login`, which asks for the code and the 2FA password and stores the session in the `tdlib-data` volume, then start the service again. Use a separate account, not a personal one.
- Without `TELEGRAM_API_ID` / `TELEGRAM_API_HASH` the service starts and does nothing; without a session it starts, logs one error and `IngestUnauthorized` fires.
- Per-chat settings come from one variable: `TELEGRAM_CHATS=zagubleno911=Львів;other_chat=Kraków|PL|pl` (`username=city[|region[|locale]]`). A Lviv channel whose posts name only a crossroads needs its city here, or Places will not find the place.
- To try the channel out, run `site,channel` against a test site with `TELEGRAM_PUBLISH_CHAT` set to a private chat, then point it at the real channel.
- To see where a post is stuck, set `LOGGING_LEVEL_ORG_SHPYTCHUK_INGEST_PIPELINE=DEBUG`: the pipeline then logs every step with its duration (queued, phone, duplicate check, each photo's download and redaction, the model answer, the checks, the place search, the upload). The last line of a post names the last finished step; the next one is the slow one. Every external call has a timeout (TDLib 30 s, model `OPENROUTER_TIMEOUT` per attempt, Places, Client-API), OCR does not.
- Exactly one instance: the TDLib session cannot be shared.
- The service needs RabbitMQ (`RABBITMQ_*`) for [notices from the site](#notices-from-the-site); without a broker the listener keeps reconnecting and the pipeline works as before.
- Facebook: log in to a separate account in a browser, open `www.facebook.com`, copy the `Cookie` request header (DevTools, Network) into `FACEBOOK_COOKIES`, set `FACEBOOK_USER_AGENT` to that browser's user agent and list the groups in `FACEBOOK_GROUPS=zzter=Тернопіль;...`. The cookies are a password: `env.test` / `env.prod` only. Without them the source logs one warning and reads nothing; when they stop logging in, `FacebookUnauthorized` fires and they have to be copied again.

Every variable is in [environment variables](../deployment/environment-variables.md).

`derechi.ingest.posts{outcome}`, `derechi.ingest.phone.source{source}`, `derechi.ingest.llm.duration{model}`, `derechi.ingest.llm.cost{model}` (USD), `derechi.ingest.image.redacted{mode}`, `derechi.ingest.published{publisher,outcome}`, `derechi.ingest.channel{action,outcome}` (notices from the site: `posted`, `edited` or `deleted`, `ok`, `skipped` or `failed`), `derechi.ingest.queue`, `derechi.ingest.postponed`, `derechi.ingest.telegram.authorized`, `derechi.ingest.facebook.authorized`, `derechi.ingest.facebook.polls{group,outcome}`, `derechi.ingest.last.received` (epoch seconds). Alerts `IngestUnauthorized`, `FacebookUnauthorized` and `IngestStalled`, see [monitoring](../deployment/monitoring.md).

## Known gaps

- Edits and deletions of the source post are not followed.
- A notice needs a phone number: a post with only a Telegram username whose owner hides the phone is dropped, although the person is reachable. Client-API requires a phone today.
- OCR corrects quarter turns and a skew of 0.5° to 20°, not perspective: a phone number or card number on a sharply angled photo can stay unblurred. A document is still caught by its portrait.
- Outside documents faces are not blurred.
- WebP photos are not decoded (the JDK has no WebP reader); Telegram serves photos as JPEG.
- The in-memory queue and the username cache are lost on restart (the catch-up refills the queue).
- A publisher that failed after `site` succeeded (`channel`, say) is not retried; the channel post has to be made by hand.
- Facebook is read through the web client's undocumented feed query: a change on their side shows up as `derechi.ingest.facebook.polls{outcome="failed"}` until the reader is fixed. The account can be banned, and a datacenter IP meets login walls sooner than a home one. Facebook posts have no author phone, so a post without a number in its text is dropped.
